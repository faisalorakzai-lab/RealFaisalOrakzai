01 / THE LONG VIEWTHE STORY

A life in service
of the next frontier.

Jensen Huang’s story is not a solo act. It is a story of teams, researchers, developers and a computing platform that changed what people could attempt.

Born in Taipei in 1963, Huang has recalled that his family moved first to Thailand and later to the United States during his childhood. He studied electrical engineering, worked in the semiconductor industry and, with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem, co-founded NVIDIA in 1993. Over decades, the company moved from graphics toward general-purpose accelerated computing—an evolution that later proved consequential for AI.

This account separates verifiable milestones from interpretation. It recognizes Huang’s impact without suggesting he personally created every breakthrough built on the tools NVIDIA helped develop.

01

A journey that began far from Silicon Valley

Jen-Hsun “Jensen” Huang was born in Taiwan in 1963. As a child, he moved to the United States. The experience of adapting across cultures became part of the public story of an engineer whose career would unfold in one of the world’s most demanding industries. In a 2024 oral-history interview, he described moving from Taipei to Thailand and then to the United States, including periods apart from his parents. The chronology is a retrospective account, so this page avoids assigning a more exact age or date than the records support. Hear the Computer History Museum oral history ↗

READ MORE: NVIDIA leadership biography ↗

02

Humble beginnings

Huang has described Denny’s as his first job: he worked as a dishwasher and was later promoted to busboy. The Stanford Graduate School of Business published his account in a 2024 interview. That experience is often cited as an early lesson in service and discipline. The details here are kept restrained: the point is not to turn one job into a fable, but to recognize how practical work can shape a leader’s habits. Read the Stanford interview ↗

His career is a reminder that a start in life does not set a limit on what a person can learn or build.

03

The engineer with a different vision

Huang earned a B.S. in electrical engineering from Oregon State University in 1984 and an M.S. in electrical engineering from Stanford in 1992, which he completed while working full time. His training connected circuit-level engineering with a broader understanding of how computing systems are designed, scaled and used.

Engineering does not predict a company’s future, but it can help leaders ask which technical constraints are fundamental—and which might be redesigned.

READ MORE: Oregon State University ↗ · NVIDIA biography ↗

04

The birth of NVIDIA

On April 5, 1993, Huang joined electrical engineers Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem to co-found NVIDIA. Their early focus was accelerated graphics for personal computers. Building a semiconductor business meant committing to long product cycles, high research costs and markets that could change before a chip reached customers.

The company’s history is a story of repeated technical bets and hard turns—not a straight line from a founding meeting to today’s AI systems.

READ MORE: NVIDIA corporate timeline ↗

05

The GPU revolution

NVIDIA launched GeForce 256 in August 1999 and described it as the first GPU; the company later dates the card’s retail release to October 11, 1999. In 2006, NVIDIA introduced CUDA as a programming platform for general-purpose GPU computing. The GPU’s many parallel operations made it valuable beyond rendering images. In 2006, CUDA gave software developers a way to program NVIDIA GPUs for general-purpose parallel computing.

CUDA did not alone create modern AI. It helped make GPU acceleration accessible to researchers and developers, and the ecosystem grew through software, hardware, libraries and years of research.

READ MORE: NVIDIA corporate timeline ↗ · CUDA overview ↗

06

From graphics to artificial intelligence

In 2012, AlexNet showed how deep convolutional neural networks trained on GPUs could advance large-scale image recognition. The research was by Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton; it was a research-team result, not a personal accomplishment of Huang. It was a landmark in the rise of deep learning—not a breakthrough attributable to one executive or one company.

GPU-accelerated computing became a useful foundation for training and deploying larger models. Researchers and engineering teams across universities, startups and technology firms built on that platform.

READ MORE: Original AlexNet paper ↗

07

Building the infrastructure of the AI era

NVIDIA develops accelerators, systems, networking and software used in AI, robotics, autonomous systems, scientific computing, healthcare research, high-performance computing and digital twins. In 2016, it announced DGX-1 as an integrated deep-learning system for research and development. The company’s platforms are part of a wider ecosystem that includes cloud operators, open-source projects, universities, labs and independent developers.

The scale of that transformation is real. So are the responsibilities around cost, energy, safety, access and concentration of power. Recognition and scrutiny belong in the same conversation.

READ MORE: NVIDIA industry overview ↗

08

Leadership, vision and persistence

Huang led NVIDIA through successive eras of graphics, programmable GPUs, accelerated computing and AI. His public leadership is associated with technical engagement, ambitious roadmaps and an unusually long tenure in an industry where technologies and markets can change rapidly.

That record invites admiration, but it is not a substitute for governance, transparency or accountability. A leader’s work should be assessed alongside the people and institutions who make the work possible.

09
CURRENT DEVELOPMENTS · FACT-CHECKED UPDATE

A difficult new chapter

On November 12, 2026, NVIDIA announced that its board had accepted Huang’s resignation as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. The company named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO while beginning a search for a permanent successor. Read NVIDIA’s announcement ↗

NVIDIA’s announcement did not state a reason. Same-day reporting by Reuters ↗ and NPR ↗ discussed allegations about personal relationships and possible security concerns. These remain allegations reported by those outlets, not findings established by the company’s statement; NPR noted it had not independently confirmed some underlying reports. This page does not infer that any allegation caused the resignation.

Professional contribution, private conduct, corporate governance and accountability are separate questions. Allegations should be attributed to the original publication and evaluated on their own evidence.

10

Why we stand with Jensen

We are not saying any leader is above criticism. Accountability matters. Transparency matters. Truth matters.

We also believe it is fair to recognize decades of work that helped give researchers, engineers, entrepreneurs and developers new tools for computing. Huang’s journey is one part of a larger history of people who made the AI era possible.

We stand for accessible innovation, technological progress and the freedom to build. We stand for an AI future where the next important idea can come from anywhere—and where opportunity is not reserved for a handful of powerful institutions.

That is the spirit behind this tribute: recognition without blind loyalty, and ambition with room for accountability.
